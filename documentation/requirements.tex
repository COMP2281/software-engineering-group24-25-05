\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Overview and Justification}

\noindent The purpose of this project is to develop an engaging educational game, based on the CounterSpy universe, that integrates IBM Skills Build content into its gameplay. This approach transforms their current educational material into an immersive, game-based experience, enhancing both learner engagement and knowledge retention; In doing so, the project addresses the challenge of making educational content more appealing and interactive, something that IBM have identified as a key area for improvement. \\

\noindent The project benefits individuals who currently learn via the IBM Skills Build, including self-learners and educational institutions seeking interactive tools to complement their curricula. By gamifying the learning process, this solution can potentially expand the reach and effectiveness of IBM Skills Build programs, particularly for younger learners or those needing alternative learning methods. \\

\noindent The project’s client is IBM, represented by John McNamara, who leads IBM UK University Programs and serves as an IBM Master Inventor. IBM’s overarching goal is to increase the engagement of Skills Build content. This project aligns with IBM’s strategic aim to integrate cutting-edge solutions into its educational offerings.

\subsection{Project Scope}

\subsubsection{Purpose and Overall Goals}

\noindent The primary goal of the project is to enhance learner engagement and retention by embedding IBM Skills Build concepts into immersive gameplay. This project addresses the growing need for innovative educational tools that effectively combine learning with interactive storytelling. \\

\noindent By integrating gamified mechanics with educational content, the project aims to create an engaging learning experience that fosters understanding and application of key concepts. The ultimate objective is to make learning enjoyable, accessible, and impactful for diverse user groups.

\subsubsection{Stakeholders}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Primary Stakeholder:} IBM, represented by John McNamara, who leads IBM UK University Programs.
    \item \textbf{Target Users:}
          \begin{itemize}
              \item Students pursuing IBM Skills Build certifications as a supplement to their academic studies.
              \item Self-learners aiming to enhance their skills through certifications in areas like AI, cybersecurity, and data analytics.
          \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Boundaries}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Educational Content:} The game is centred on IBM Skills Build content, deliberately avoiding irrelevant gameplay elements, such as worldbuilding. The focus is on creating a balance between engagement and educational value.

    \item \textbf{Platform Compatibility:} Development is restricted to Windows due to resource and time constraints. Expanding to additional platforms, such as mobile devices or gaming consoles, is beyond the project's scope.

    \item \textbf{Multiplayer:} The game will support only single-player gameplay to maintain simplicity and focus on consistent educational content delivery. Implementing multiplayer modes is excluded from the current scope.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Vision}

\noindent \textit{Shadow Operative} aspires to combine stealth gameplay mechanics with dynamic, question-based educational challenges. By immersing players in an engaging narrative, the game aims to inspire new, interactive approaches to learning. \\

\noindent The project envisions a future where educational tools seamlessly integrate entertainment and learning, driving a new standard for impactful and enjoyable education.

\subsection{System Description}

\subsubsection{System Overview}

\noindent Shadow Operative is an educational game designed to help learners engage with IBM Skills Build content in an immersive and interactive environment. The game places players in the role of a spy during the Cold War, tasked with retrieving stolen plans for a cutting-edge AI system. Through skill-based challenges and AI-driven adaptive difficulty, players are guided through progressively complex levels that reinforce core IBM Skills Build concepts in a gamified format.

\subsubsection{Research into Alternative Solutions}

\noindent When designing Shadow Operative, the team conducted extensive research into comparable games and educational platforms to inform and enhance its structure, mechanics, and learning approach. The following games served as key references, with their unique aspects and usefulness to Shadow Operative outlined below:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{CounterSpy:} A Cold War-era side-scrolling stealth game, CounterSpy serves as a primary thematic reference for Shadow Operative, offering insight into effective use of stealth elements, setting, and art direction within a Cold War context.
          \begin{itemize}
              \item \textbf{Limitation:} CounterSpy does not integrate educational content, which limits its application in learning environments. Shadow Operative addresses this by embedding educational challenges into the gameplay.
          \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{This War Of Mine:} This side-scrolling survival game provides a perspective on civilian experiences of war, integrating stealth mechanics and adding depth to our Cold War setting by informing character interactions and emotional undertones.
          \begin{itemize}
              \item \textbf{Limitation:} The game's heavy emotional themes may not be suitable or relevant for younger learners. Shadow Operative maintains engagement while ensuring content is appropriate for educational purposes.
          \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Kahoot!:} This gamified quiz platform highlights the power of interactive question-based challenges and adaptive difficulty. It directly influences Shadow Operative's question generation system and difficulty adjustments, with timers and music used to create urgency in an educational context.
          \begin{itemize}
              \item \textbf{Limitation:} Kahoot!'s approach lacks the narrative and immersive elements we aim to include, which Shadow Operative addresses by integrating learning into a cohesive story-driven experience.
          \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Proposed Solution’s Technical Features}

\noindent Shadow Operative integrates the following key technical features to create a unique, adaptive learning experience:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Question Generation:} Relevant IBM Skills Build content will populate various levels, embedding knowledge assessments seamlessly into gameplay scenarios. Questions will adapt in difficulty and context to align with players' progress, enhancing engagement and reinforcing learning objectives.

    \item \textbf{Dynamic Difficulty Adjustment:} AI algorithms will monitor and assess player performance, adjusting the difficulty and types of questions asked throughout the game. This ensures players are consistently engaged and challenged without being overwhelmed, fostering a tailored learning experience.

    \item \textbf{Boss Interactions:} Key encounters, such as boss battles, act as knowledge assessments. Players demonstrate their understanding of core IBM Skills Build concepts in a high-stakes, interactive format that reinforces learning.

    \item \textbf{Stealth Mechanics} Inspired by CounterSpy, Shadow Operative incorporates stealth elements like sneaking, cover mechanics, and enemy awareness to create a tense, immersive experience that complements the game's educational content, keeping the user engaged.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Integration Considerations}

% \noindent Shadow Operative is designed as a standalone educational game; however, it is making use of IBM’s AI technology, Granite, allowing the user to make the experience adaptive to them, focusing more on their use-case of the system.

\noindent Shadow Operative is designed to be a standalone educational game, but
also integrates with IBM's AI technology, Granite. Granite allows the game to
dynamically adapt to the user's skill level, learning objectives and progress,
creating a more personalized and engaging experience.

\section{Solution Requirements}

\subsection{Requirements Elicitation}

\noindent To gain a thorough understanding of the client’s needs, our team reached out via email on 15\textsuperscript{th} October 2024, aiming to address specific concerns about feature integration, gameplay mechanics, and AI-driven educational content. The client responded on the 17\textsuperscript{th} October 2024, clarifying the project’s focus on seamlessly incorporating IBM Skills Build content (including AI, Cybersecurity, and Data Analytics) into the gameplay. This exchange allowed us to refine the project scope, confirm the inclusion of adaptive learning through AI algorithms, and remove features identified as redundant. These insights also guided us in structuring team roles based on each member’s strengths and development areas.

\subsubsection{Challenges Encountered}

\noindent The first challenge we encountred was that of organizing our first
meeting with the client. Conflicting schedules among team members caused a delay
in setting up a direct conversation, which impacted our early progress.
Additionally, one of our team members was unavailable for a few weeks, which
further complicated scheduling and resource allocation. Despite these obstacles,
the team coordinated through asynchronous communication and adjusted roles
temporarily to maintain momentum. \\

\noindent Following this, we held an internal meeting to discuss our objectives in depth and to identify how each member’s skills could contribute to creating a compelling, interactive experience that aligns with CounterSpy’s Cold War-inspired, side-scrolling stealth gameplay. This meeting helped us generate additional questions for the client regarding elements such as how best to implement game mechanics that motivate players to learn and progress through adaptive, question-based challenges. We also explored design inspirations, focusing on essential aspects like gameplay mechanics, Cold War-era visual style, and strategies to maintain a consistent thematic experience. During this meeting, we reached a consensus that the card feature did not align with the game’s objectives, which we then proposed for removal in a follow-up email. The client agreed with this decision.

\subsubsection{Requirement Validation}

\noindent To ensure that our refined requirements aligned accurately with the client’s needs, we sent a confirmation email summarizing key decisions and requested feedback. The client’s positive response affirmed our direction. We also planned a follow-up meeting to further validate our approach and address any remaining questions, ensuring continuous alignment with the client’s educational goals. \\

\noindent Our team then met to discuss the client’s feedback and refine our understanding of the project’s requirements. We identified key features, such as question generation, dynamic difficulty adjustment, and boss interactions, all of which would enhance the game’s educational value and align with the client’s objectives.

\section{Project Management}

\subsection{Risks and Issues}

\noindent When developing a large software project with a team, it is important to be aware of the potential risks and issues that may arise, and to have plans in place to mitigate them. Issues may arise from a variety of sources, including the group itself, the client, the software produced or even the available hardware.

\subsubsection{Group Risks}

\noindent One of the most significant risks when working in a team is that of team members not working together effectively. This may result from differing levels of knowledge, different problem solving styles, alternate programming styles and more. Fortunately, this can be mitigated by creating comprehensive plans for the project and ensuring everyone is assigned roles that suit their strengths. If the team cannot work together effectively, it can become difficult to develop a cohesive product, and may lead to more bugs and issues down the line.

\noindent Uneven workloads, despite being unfair on those doing more work, can lead to some members not understanding the current state of the project or how new features should be implemented. This can make it hard to continue contributing to the project, resulting in even lower productivity, slower development and a less complete product. To mitigate this risk, it is important to ensure that everyone is assigned tasks before the project starts, and that regular meetings are held to discuss progress and potential task shifts.

\subsubsection{Incremental Model}

\noindent The Incremental Model divides the project into smaller, manageable portions (increments). Each increment represents a subset of the full game functionality. \\

\noindent With each increment, you develop a part of the game and deliver it. Once that part is functional, you can move on to the next one. \\

\noindent Each new increment builds upon the previous one, allowing you to add new features, mechanics, or puzzle elements step by step. \\

\noindent Risk is managed effectively by focusing on simpler, high-priority features early and addressing complex ones later.

\subsubsection{Application in the Project Context}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Gradual Progress:} Our game comprises multiple components, including core mechanics (e.g., running, jumping, and sliding), puzzles, and IBM Skills Build badge questions. Using the Incremental Model, we can develop each component sequentially, starting with the foundational mechanics before progressing to puzzles and gamification elements like the card system.

    \item \textbf{Iterative Feedback and Testing:} After completing each increment, we can conduct testing and gather client or player feedback. This ensures each feature works as intended and aligns with the project goals before integrating it into the overall game.

    \item \textbf{Risk Management:} By addressing straightforward features early in the development process, the Incremental Model reduces risk and ensures a consistent development pace. This strategy allows us to allocate more time and resources to resolving challenges posed by complex features.

    \item \textbf{Flexibility for Changes:} Given the possibility of evolving client requirements or necessary adjustments, the Incremental Model minimizes disruption. Changes can be accommodated without requiring significant rework, as only specific increments need updating, preserving the integrity of prior developments.

    \item \textbf{Early Player Engagement:} The model allows us to release a playable version of the game before full completion. Early releases enable us to collect valuable feedback from players and the client, refining the game’s direction and functionality based on real-world input.

    \item \textbf{Adaptability to a Small Team:} With a team of seven, the Incremental Model facilitates efficient workload distribution. Team members can focus on specific tasks within each increment, ensuring all participants are engaged and progress is clearly tracked.
\end{enumerate}

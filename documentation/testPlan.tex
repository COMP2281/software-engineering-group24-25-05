\section{Introduction and Test Overview}

\noindent Shadow Operative is an educational video game that integrates IBM SkillsBuild content to create an engaging learning platform focused on AI, Cybersecurity, and Data Analytics. The game incorporates IBM SkillsBuild badges and leverages AI-driven adaptive learning to personalise content based on players' performance and progress. This innovative approach combines education with entertainment, fostering a dynamic and enjoyable learning experience. \\

\noindent Players can earn industry-recognized certifications by completing practical, game-based challenges that build valuable skills in high-demand areas. The game dynamically adjusts its difficulty and content to cater to individual learning paths, promoting mastery of complex topics, and encouraging continuous growth. \\

\noindent This testing report presents a comprehensive evaluation of Shadow Operative, including the following key sections:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Testing Scope} – Defines the extent and objectives of the testing process.
    \item \textbf{Test Cases} – Describes specific scenarios and conditions evaluated during testing.
    \item \textbf{Testing Content} – Outlines the areas and characteristics subjected to testing, along with key findings and results.
\end{itemize}

\noindent The report highlights critical insights, performance evaluations, and improvements, demonstrating the educational value of Shadow Operative.

\subsubsection{Enemy AI Behaviour}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}m{4cm}|X|}
    \hline
    \textbf{Test ID} & utest\_4 \\
    \hline
    \textbf{Description of test} & This test case verifies the behaviour of the enemy AI in a game environment, specifically focusing on their patrol and detection routines. The AI should follow a set patrol path and detect the player within a specified detection radius. \\
    \hline
    \textbf{Related requirement document details} & \makecell[l] {
        AI Boss (2.2.4) \\
        Combat Mechanics (2.2.9)
    } \\
    \hline
    \textbf{Prerequisites for test} & \makecell[l] {
        Enemy prefab created with AI controller. \\
        Test environment with patrol routes and obstacles to test pathfinding.
    } \\
    \hline
    \textbf{Test procedure} &
    \textbf{Test enemy patrol behaviour}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Place the enemy in the environment with a predefined patrol route.
        \item Observe the enemy moving smoothly between patrol points while scanning for the player within its field of view.
        \item Ensure the enemy turns around or adjusts its patrol when it reaches the end of the route or encounters a barrier.
    \end{itemize}
    \textbf{Test enemy detection of the player}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Position the player within the enemy’s field of view.
        \item Verify the enemy enters a detection state when the player is visible.
        \item Ensure the detection is consistent with the game’s design (e.g., player being fully visible vs. partially obscured).
    \end{itemize}
    \textbf{Test edge cases for player visibility}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Position the player at partially obscured locations (e.g., just outside the enemy’s field of view or behind thin obstacles).
        \item Verify that the enemy does not detect the player unless they are within the intended visibility range or in line of sight.
        \item Confirm that if the player moves quickly through the enemy’s field of view, the enemy reacts briefly before resuming its patrol.
    \end{itemize}
    \textbf{Test reaction to unreachable players}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Place the player in positions the enemy cannot reach (e.g., higher platforms or behind barriers).
        \item Ensure the enemy does not attempt impossible paths to reach the player.
        \item Verify the enemy either paces in frustration, searches nearby, or returns to its patrol route after a delay.
    \end{itemize}
    \\
    \hline
    \textbf{Test material used} & Unity Console Logs, Unity Gizmos to see Raycasts \\
    \hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\section{Testing Context}

\noindent To ensure compatibility, accessibility, and coverage across educational environments, the following tools and environments are used for testing the system:

\subsection{Operating Systems}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Windows 11: Selected due to its widespread use in educational institutions and professional environments. Ensures compatibility for schools and educational labs. According to recent statistics, Microsoft Windows holds a market share of approximately 66.7\% in the UK, reflecting its dominance in both educational and professional settings \cite{statista}.
    \item macOS (15.3 and above): Included as many schools and creative institutions prefer macOS systems for educational and development purposes. macOS accounts for around 23.3\% of the UK market share, demonstrating its popularity in environments that emphasise creative and media-focused curricula \cite{statista}.
\end{itemize}

The dominance of these two operating systems influences system testing priorities. Ensuring compatibility with both Windows and macOS is crucial to accommodate the technological landscape within educational environments. Given their built-in accessibility features (such as screen readers, magnification tools, and input customisation), both platforms support diverse user needs, which are integral to accessibility testing.

\subsection{Testing Tools and Drivers}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Unity Editor (2022.3.50f1): Used to test and debug game mechanics, AI behaviours, and user interactions.
    \item Accessibility Testing Tools: Simulators for colourblindness and screen reader compatibility (e.g., Color Oracle, VoiceOver for macOS). According to Keywords Studios, around 29\% of gamers in the UK identify as having a disability, and 66\% of these players report encountering barriers in gaming environments \cite{keywordsstudios}. Ensuring accessibility testing mitigates these barriers and promotes inclusivity.
    \item Input Device Testing: Keyboard and controller compatibility tests using standard USB and wireless game controllers. This includes accessibility-focused input devices, such as adaptive controllers, to accommodate players with limited mobility. Scope UK highlights the importance of these options for players with varying needs \cite{scope}.
    \item Game Feature Accessibility Testing: Incorporates tests for adjustable difficulty settings, subtitles, and customisable controls, which are essential for ensuring inclusivity and usability for a wide audience, including players with and without disabilities. The global importance of accessibility in gaming underscores the necessity for these considerations \cite{keywordsstudios}.
\end{itemize}

The technological landscape from 2017 to 2023 demonstrates the sustained dominance of Windows and macOS, with Linux and other open-source systems remaining less adopted due to compatibility and software availability concerns. As accessibility continues to gain prominence, the integration of sophisticated accessibility features across these platforms is expected to become more comprehensive and integral to development and testing strategies.
